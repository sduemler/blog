### Neural DSP

I feel like I rediscovered childhood excitement when Neural DSP announced that John Mayer had released an archetype with them. Neural DSP is an amp modeler and effects company that allows you to plug a guitar straight into a computer instead of having to go through an amp and lets you pretend that you have a full pedalboard. I have an real amp that has a bunch of built in effects and presets, but most of the just make you sound like Dime Bag Darrell got drunker than he had ever been before.

I started out with the Cory Wong Archetype when they released it because while I had known about Neural before, most of their preset offerings were for prog rock and metal like Tim Henson from Polyphia. Just plug and play, almost no bad sounds to be found. I was so excited about I immediately threw it on every track I was working on. Then to my surprise, a few weeks later a little teaser popped up on my YouTube homepage showing that the John Mayer one had just released. Mayer sculpted much of who I would become as a guitar player, and the only reason I haven’t ever bought his $2500 signature PRS guitar is because I don’t have the $20,000 boutique amp setup required to make it sound good.

One of the cool things about each of the Archetypes is Neural gets a bunch of other artists to create presets from the pedals and amps offered in the package. And it’s not always ones that you think will fit either, for example Bear McCreary, who wrote the score for all of the new God of War games, has a few presets using John’s tech.

For someone who only ever had a looper pedal before this, this being priced at the price point of ONE PEDAL still blows my mind. Look out for it all over my music!

### Wokyis Mac Dock

I forget how I came across this one, but I had to wait so long for it that it barely made the list for 2025. Ordered off a Kickstarter back in August, I only received it a week ago. It’s made in Hong Kong so maybe they had bigger issues at hand to deal with, but I’ve loved it ever since I got it! I had small Satechi bottom dock for my Mac Mini M4 previous to this that I was able to throw an NVME SSD in to double the storage , as well as a bunch of extra ports. Rather than just getting placed underneath, the Wokyis is placed completely over the Mac like an enclosure, leaving the necessary front and back ports open for use still. It has a smaller than imagined 5 in. screen that can be toggled on and off.

I’ve mostly been using it for small fullscreen Spotify pictured well by Remi Wolf here. Once my OSRS addiction comes back around I anticipate that it will get some good use out of it as well. I enjoy only having 1 screen for my setup instead of 2 massive ones, but there always moments that were annoying to share screen space. The Wokyis is such a cute and useful addition to the desk setup!

### Dry Needling

I have always been jealous of the people who can come out of a massage or PT or simply some light stretching feeling like a new person and much reduced pain. I’ve always felt like a rock that just gets little chips knocked off by a massage gun or yoga, but largely staying the same including tension and pain. Dry needling has changed the game for me. There’s a physical therapist connected with the gym that I go to, and she was the one who first introduced me to the process after a nagging knee injury took me out of squatting for a bit, which we just cannot have.

While the process and about a day thereafter can be a lot more painful than the injury at rest, the following days are just magical. Imagine acupuncture but the it’s only one needle and the acupuncturist really has it out for you. Who knew jamming a needle in a person (very skillfully) and twisting it around a muscle (also skillfully hopefully) could relieve so much of the tension built up in it. Most recently she did some dry needling work on my traps, and it has really helped clear up some of my recurring tension headaches.

### omg.lol

I’m a big advocator for everyone having their own personal space on the web, and [omg.lol](http://omg.lol) provides a good middle step in between nothing or only social media accounts and having your own website. While it does depend on the owner of omg.lol retaining the service, it lets you create a profile out of markdown or pure HTML with a few small guardrails, reminiscent of MySpace. You could stop there to have something similar to a Linktree, albeit with more personalization.

Though if you continue behind the scenes, you gain access to a ton of small tools that you usually have to use all different services for. A handy pastebin to share code snippets, a way to create PURLs, email forwarding, a DNS service, and few more that are less useful except in very specific situations. For just $20 a year, I think it’s absolutely worth it. It’s a fantastic way for people to test out the waters of having their own space on the web. \<a href=”[https://samd.omg.lol/”](https://samd.omg.lol/%E2%80%9D)>Here’s mine!\</a>

### porkbun

Up until this point, my URL ownership had become more and more spread out as I worked on different projects or found better tools to use. Starting out with AWS in college, a few Squarespace tries here and there, Google Domains to snag a .dev URL (then having to move those domains over to Squarespace after Google took Domains back behind the barn, shot it, and threw it on the trash pile of all the other “failed” projects), then adding a couple to Netlify for ease of use with their deployments.

Once I started using [omg.lol](http://omg.lol) I wanted to redirect my [samduemler.com](http://samduemler.com) URL to my omg.lol profile. I had it parked in AWS, and after a couple hours of poking around docs, I found that the only way  to do a proper redirect from AWS to a different service, you had to create an S3 bucket and add rules in there, which felt like far too much to simply do [samduemler.com](http://samduemler.com) → samd.omg.lol. Most other services like Squarespace and Netlify have much easier ways to do it.

### Renaissance Fair

Ever since high school I have entertained the idea of going to a Renaissance Fair. Growing up with mostly fantasy books surrounding me, it seemed like the perfect place to bring some of those pictures to life. I came close at various times, but never quite was able to pull the trigger on any of them. This past October I finally did it with my partner and some friends! Looking back I’m kind of happy that it turned out this way, because I didn’t have any disposable income any of the other chances that I had to go and I quickly learned that having that is very important to a large portion of the experience. Not that I’m complaining, I love all of the little trinkets and baubles that I brought back as part of the experience. But dressing up a little elf (more like a hobbit) with a bunch of my friends, seeing knights crash into each other, having trained birds fly right over me, and embarrassing myself by not hitting the bell on the hammer game are all the things that I won’t forget.

### Geese band

It’s been quite some time that I’ve fallen for a band as hard I fell into the Geese hole for all of December. According to [Last.fm](http://last.fm), which is unfortunately the only way I have to keep track of this, I listened to Geese’s latest album Getting Killed 205 times over the course of 30 days. I first saw them mentioned in the Track Star show on YouTube where one of their tracks was being shown to Billy Corgan (who seemed nonplussed at best).“ It took me a few listen throughs to really get into it. At first it seemed sloppy, like someone had slapped a SM57 in the middle of a garage for a high school alt rock band rehearsal. Husbands” was the first song that really grabbed me, and from there the whole album opened up for me. I came to appreciate that sloppiness, showing that it truly doesn’t matter the quality as long as it has feeling and is a good song. Cameron Winter’s voice somehow feels lazy but also jam packed with emotion, verging on tears at points. I can’t decide if it’s effortless or him trying his best, but whatever it is I love it.

In the car, working, on the plane back to Nashville for the holidays, Geese was my soundtrack for my December.

### mymind

This has been one of my favorite discoveries this year. I have only been using it for a few months, but I feel like it’s the tool that I’ve been wanting for years now. Before this tool every time I would come across a cool website or piece of art or article or thing I wanted to buy I would have to do the “Worth It” equation in my head, where I had to do the calculation of the thing was worth it to dedicate an entire bookmark to. I know this is silly since bookmarks are not a limited resource, but I like to keep my bookmarks tidy and organized. Notion and Instapaper solved a few of these issues for me, but I feel like I’m saving so many more things now that I can press a button in my browser or share it on my phone. I can clean out the space in my actual mind that was being used to do those calculations and just save something if it made me slow down just for a second to look at it. Cool new item from the MoMa store? Saved. Found an online file converter that isn’t just malware? Saved. Picture of pikachu saying he should believe in himself more? Saved.
