I am a generational hater. I love to hate. Chief among them has been paying for small, inane things that do nothing to improve an experience. Some examples within this, at least in tech, would be buying ringtones (in the before times), or buying wallpapers, which I have been tempted to do. But at least now, in an age of AI, I have very little trust for someone selling wallpapers. Even phone apps, since there are so many free versions of things. After becoming a developer myself and gaining some disposable income, I have softened on this stance a bit, but really only for utilities like better weather info or guided meditations.

However, there has been an app recently that I purchased that goes against all of this, one that I hate to love: the [Klack](https://tryklack.com/) app. An app that makes your keyboard on your MacBook sound like you’re typing on a mechanical keyboard. I hated the words that I just typed out, but damn if they didn’t sound good when I did it.

first found out about this beautiful waste of money through looking into getting a different app called [Alcove](https://tryalcove.com/), an app that adds functionality to the cutout at the top of current-day MacBooks. I was able to resist the siren song of that one since it didn’t really fit my use case, but linked in the demo for it was this other app called Klack. No real use case, certainly less than any amount of utility that Alcove might have brought me, but once I heard a couple of the clicky clackies of the keyboard sounds available for Klack, it took me all but a couple contemplative sips of coffee that cost more than the app did ($5) to hand over my credit card info to add some constant sound to my computer work.

I’ve had my time in the sun with mechanical keyboards and have my requisite stack of them in my closet gathering dust between the keys. I’ve made my way through a couple of the Logitech G series, wore out a Glorious mechanical when they were still known as “Glorious PC Gaming Race”, spent far, far too much money on building my own from Massdrop with a keyboard set that used Nordic runes, which was useless whenever I needed to find the right key, and finally tested out a few Keychron boards before settling on a Keychron Q1 with a knob. I’m largely retired from the mechanical keyboard building/buying scene as much as you can be, but I’m still very much into the sound and feel and use of them.

I’m pleasantly content with the feel of the current MacBook keyboards after their rejection of the butterfly design that was so controversial. However, for any typing tasks longer than a tweet, I find myself gravitating towards the mechanical keyboard at my desk. Klack has evened the playing field a bit. Boot up Bongo Cat as my little typing companion in the corner of my screen and crank the sounds of my Klack app and I’m off to the races without my real mechanical keyboard. Cardboard is the sound pack I’m rocking with right now, though I’m also partial to the Milky Yellow or Cream. I much prefer the depth of them compared to the wimpy Crystal Purple, Japanese Black, or Oreo sounds.

When I bought the app I thought, “Ah maybe I’ll get a few days’ use out of this, or use it every once in a while and then forget about it and those five bucks will be gone.” I haven’t stopped using it since I got it, except to be a polite coffee shop customer. I’ve even tried to use it in headphones while working at a coffee shop, but the lag between the keystroke and the sound with Bluetooth is horrendous.

I’m always playing music whenever I’m working, but sometimes when I’m writing even instrumentals use up the creative part of my brain and gum up the words from coming out. Klack gives me a nice middle ground of a kind of white noise in the background that can keep my mind from wandering too far, but also subtle enough that I’m not trying to decipher where a song might go next. It also means I don’t look like a psychopath who just listens to static so that I can focus.

I hate that I love it this much, but at this point I don’t think I have any grounds to deny it. It always makes me a little more excited to type something out long form, albeit to a lesser degree than a real mechanical keyboard. I love this silly little app, and I can’t wait to try out any new keyboard types that get added to it.
