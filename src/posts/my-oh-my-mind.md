I listed the service myMind as one of my favorite things to come out of 2025 for me, and I want to explain more as to why. I have long had an ever growing list of bookmarks and folders in my browsers, 90% of which I haven't visited since I bookmarked them initially. This has continually proved to be a thorn in my side whenever trying out a new browser (currently Vivaldi, which I'm not sold on yet) since the port process has never been as simple as the new browser proposes.

The other issue with the bookmark system is that every time I found a product or a site or an image that I liked and wanted to go back to eventually, I had to do the mental math in my head for if it was worth denoting a bookmark for and growing the already overfed categorization of URLs. I'm sure that I've lost a few things into the ether of the web by deciding not to. As a digital hoarder, this pains me. Truthfully speaking it's highly unlikely that any of these lost sites would have changed my life in any way, other than clicking on it out of curiosity next time I switch browsers and thinking "ah yeah I remember why I saved that".

myMind has solved this problem for me. I find a cool small web site (like [tinywebplayground](https://tinywebplayground.com) check it out it'll make you say "neat") that I will likely never go back to but still want to save it and forget it? Just press the little orange + sign in my toolbar, and it's in myMind forever! Like an image of a robot cowboy? Don't worry about it taking up space on your hard drive, myMind will save it! Found a lamp that is far too expensive to warrant buying? Save it and torture yourself and if you really should get it every time you look at your myMind page!

Being able to scroll through a single page and see striations of what you were interested at a given time instead of having to click through bookmark folders and trying to remember why you saved something removes all guilt or second guessing on if I should save something or not. It's like an archaeological dig moving through the page, uncovering the times when I was researching different microphones or was falling into the dangerous headspace of considering getting another watch.

The one complaint that I had about it was that as the amount of items in the space grows, I end up looking at the things farther back less and less. One of my other favorite things from 2025 that I listed was the Verge's weekly newsletter "Installer" which gave a few things that the editor was enjoying or looking forward to soon, as well things from the community and often a selected individual who is given a deeper dive on their interests. In order to keep things from disappearing into the back of the queue forever, I built a service that once a week, generates an email that gets sent to myself that contains 5 things that I've saved that I considered buying but haven't, 3 articles that I haven't read yet, and a 3rd section that changes every week. Some weeks it picks a random color and offers up an entry that I've saved that contains that color. Other times it picks an image that I've saved and resurfaces it for me. For example, this week it's offered up this image of a vagrant robot from one of my favorite modern artists Simon Stalenhag. I've scheduled it to arrive in my inbox every Sunday around 9 AM (the day after Installer comes out to spread out the emails I actually enjoy getting) and I look forward to it every weekend.

![](/assets/my-oh-my-mind/vagrantrobot.jpg)

They have a generous free tier of I believe 100 saves, so you can try out if it's helpful for you or just another garbage dump to put stuff that you say you're going to get back to but never do.
